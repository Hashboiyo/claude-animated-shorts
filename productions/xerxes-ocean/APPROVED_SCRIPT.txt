A Persian king once had the ocean whipped three hundred times and placed under arrest.

It's 480 BC, and King Xerxes needs to march his army across a mile of sea into Greece.

So his engineers spend months stretching two giant floating bridges from shore to shore.

Before a single boot hits the planks, a storm snaps the ropes and smashes both bridges.

A normal ruler would just rebuild, but Xerxes takes the weather personally.

He orders soldiers down to the beach to whip the waves three hundred times with chains.

Branders poke the surf with red-hot irons, and guards toss in handcuffs to place the water under arrest.

They even read an official curse out loud, calling the sea a bitter, salty little river.

Luckily for the ocean, water doesn't care about handcuffs.

Unluckily for the engineers, Xerxes has all of them beheaded.

The new engineers really liked having heads, so they anchor six hundred and seventy-four warships nose-first into the current.

They lash the hulls with braided cables, pave a road on top, and march across for seven straight days.

A year later, when Xerxes retreated from Greece, another storm wrecked the second bridge too.

So the king who handcuffed the ocean has to take a boat home like everybody else
